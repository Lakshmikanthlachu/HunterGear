Safety & regulations

Plain-language guidance on licensing, safe handling, season dates and gear care. It is a starting point, not legal advice.

Always confirm with your wildlife agency. Regulations differ by state, province, unit and year. The dates and notes on this page are samples for demonstration.

Licensing and permits

Four steps to be legal before you leave the house.

  1. 1

    Take hunter education

    Most agencies require a certified course before your first licence. Many offer online classes with a short field day.

  2. 2

    Buy your licence and tags

    Buy online or in store. Tags are species-specific, so confirm you hold one for each animal you plan to take.

  3. 3

    Check your unit’s rules

    Season dates, legal methods and bag limits change by unit. Read the current regulations booklet.

  4. 4

    Carry proof in the field

    Keep your licence, tags and ID with you. Many places accept digital copies, but carry a printout anyway.

Safe handling in the field

These four rules apply to every firearm, every time. Memorise them and repeat them to anyone you hunt with.

  1. 1

    Treat every firearm as loaded

    Even one you just checked. Open the action and look, every time you pick it up.

  2. 2

    Point the muzzle in a safe direction

    Keep it away from people, yourself and anything you don’t intend to shoot, including when you climb or cross a fence.

  3. 3

    Keep your finger off the trigger

    Rest it along the frame until the sights are on target and you’ve decided to shoot.

  4. 4

    Be sure of your target and what’s beyond it

    Identify the animal fully. Know what lies behind it before you release the shot or the arrow.

Wear blaze orange

Many areas require blaze orange during firearm seasons. Where it’s optional, wear it anyway: hunters in orange are easy to spot through brush. Check the exact requirement for your unit.

Tree stand and bow safety

Wear a full-body harness from the ground up, use a haul line for your gear and keep three points of contact on the ladder. Carry broadheads in a quiver or case, never loose.

Gear care that pays off

Ten minutes after each hunt keeps your gear working for years.

  • Optics

    Wipe lenses with a microfibre cloth, never your sleeve. Cap them and store the pair in a padded case.

  • Boots

    Brush off mud, pull the insoles and dry them away from a heater. Reproof leather before each season.

  • Bows

    Wax the string every few weeks and check the limbs and cams for cracks before opening day.

  • Knives

    Wipe the blade dry after use, add a thin film of oil and store it outside the leather sheath.

  • Camo and base layers

    Wash with scent-free detergent, air dry and keep them in a sealed bag until you head out.

  • Packs

    Empty every pocket, shake out the crumbs and dry the pack fully to prevent mildew.

Common questions

Still stuck? Ask us.

Do I need a licence to hunt with a bow?

In most places, yes. Archery hunting usually needs the same licence as other methods plus a tag for the species. Check your wildlife agency’s current regulations before you go.

What blaze orange do I need to wear?

Rules change by state, province and season. Many areas require a hat and a vest or coat during firearm seasons. Confirm the requirement with your agency, then pick gear that meets it.

Can you check my bow’s draw length and tune it?

Yes. Bring your bow to the store and we will measure, tune and test it with you at no charge.

Do you sell firearms or ammunition?

No. Ridgeline Outfitters stocks optics, apparel, archery, camping and field gear. We can point you to a licensed dealer nearby.

Can I return boots I wore outdoors?

You can wear them indoors for up to 30 days and return them. Boots with mud, sole wear or odour cannot be returned.

How do I find my hunting unit?

Use your wildlife agency’s online map, or ask us. We keep printed maps for local units at the counter.
